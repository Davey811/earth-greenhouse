The Earth Test

Earth's surface averages 288 K, 15 °C. Is that set by the pressure and the lapse rate, or by the greenhouse effect? Same planet, same sunlight, same air, same lapse rate. Two runs, and an energy ledger that has to balance.

Who does what: the published attribution

Share of the present-day greenhouse effect, GISS ModelE (Schmidt et al. 2010; Lacis et al. 2010).

Water vapour~50%
Clouds~25%
CO₂~20%
Other gases~5%

Water vapour and clouds do most of the absorbing, but they condense and rain out: their amount follows the temperature. CO₂, methane and ozone don't. In the GISS model, removing those non-condensing gases makes the water follow them out: within 50 years the global mean falls to −21 °C, 34.8 K colder, and sea ice grows from 4.6% to 46.7% of the globe.

"Other gases" is the 25% of the non-condensing gases minus CO₂'s 20%. Lacis et al. 2010, GISS research brief.

Isn't CO₂ saturated?

At the centre of its 15 µm band the air is already opaque: there, more CO₂ changes little near the ground. But Earth doesn't radiate the band to space from the ground. It radiates it from aloft, from the level where the air above is thin enough to let it out. More CO₂ lifts that level to colder air, so less leaves, until the column below warms up; the edges of the band, not yet opaque, widen too. The result is a forcing that grows with the logarithm of the concentration: every doubling adds the same, about 3.7 W/m². A saturated band would give a flat line.

With feedbacks, mostly water vapour: about 3 K per doubling, likely range 2.5–4 K (IPCC AR6), for CO₂ alone once the oceans have caught up; the warming measured so far also depends on the other gases, the aerosols and the oceans' delay.

Out of balance: the warming in progress

The runs above show a planet in balance. The real Earth isn't, quite: it absorbs more than it sends back to space, and the difference is measured from orbit and in the oceans.

The First Law, in time

Why does an imbalance last? Double CO₂ at once. Earth now radiates from 151 m higher, colder air, and sends out 3.7 W/m² less than it absorbs. That energy stays: it warms the ocean, and the surface with it, until the surface is 0.98 K warmer and the balance is back. Move the years.

No feedbacks, one heat store: the top 100 m of ocean over 71% of the planet, C = 1025 × 3990 × 100 × 0.71 = 2.90 × 10⁸ J/(m²·K). The imbalance decays as 3.71 × e−t/τ W/m², with τ = C / (4σTe³) = 2.4 years; the energy stored is C × ΔT, the area under the imbalance curve. The real ocean is deeper and slower, and feedbacks, mostly water vapour, bring the final warming to about 3 K: the full response takes decades, and meanwhile CO₂ keeps rising. That is why the measured imbalance persists. Measured values: von Schuckmann et al. 2023 (0.76 ± 0.2 W/m², 2006–2020; 381 ZJ gained 1971–2020), Loeb et al. 2021 (CERES satellites and Argo ocean floats).

CO₂ or the Sun? The stratosphere decides

A brighter Sun and more CO₂ both warm the surface. They part ways higher up. Pick one, then look at what was measured.

Measured, 1979–2018: satellites (MSU, AMSU, SSU), radiosondes and GPS radio occultation (Steiner et al. 2020). The lower stratosphere, 15–25 km, cooled too, but there the main driver until the mid-1990s was ozone loss (IPCC AR6); the clean CO₂ signal is higher up, 25–50 km, and natural causes can't explain it (Santer et al. 2023). The Sun heats the stratosphere through the ultraviolet that ozone absorbs, so a brighter Sun would warm it. Natural drivers, the Sun included: −0.1 to +0.1 °C of the warming since 1850–1900 (IPCC AR6, SPM A.1.3). Arrows show the direction, not the size. Profile: U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976.

The argument, step by step

  1. Earth is in energy balance. It absorbs about 240 W/m² of sunlight, averaged over the planet, and sends about the same back to space as infrared. The small difference, under 1 W/m², is the warming now under way.
  2. Without infrared absorbers, the surface can't be warmer than 255 K. Air that doesn't absorb infrared doesn't emit it either: the surface is the only thing that radiates, and all of its radiation escapes. So σT⁴, averaged over the surface, equals the 240 W/m² absorbed: 255 K, −18 °C, whatever the pressure, the winds, the convection or the lapse rate. A surface with hot and cold patches averages lower, not higher. Even reflecting nothing, the limit would be 278 K.
  3. The real surface emits 396 W/m², and only 240 leave. The other 156 W/m² of the surface's infrared is absorbed by the air. That is the greenhouse effect: the infrared radiation energy trapped by atmospheric gases and clouds. Raval & Ramanathan 1989 This step doesn't depend on how energy moves inside the atmosphere, only on how much leaves the surface and how much leaves Earth.
  4. The surface lives on what the air sends back. The surface absorbs about 161 W/m² of sunlight and emits 396. The air sends back down 333 W/m² of infrared, measured; the rest leaves the surface as rising warm air and evaporation. Trenberth et al. 2009; Wild et al. 2013
  5. The lapse rate fills in the shape. Earth radiates to space from about 5.1 km, where the air is at 255 K; below it the troposphere warms by 6.5 K per km: 255.02 + 6.5 × 5.097 = 288.15 K. The absorbers set the height, the lapse rate sets the slope. This step explains the profile; the 5.1 km and the slope are read from the measured profile, so 288 K comes out by construction. The proof is in steps 1 to 4.

Radiation alone would be hotter

Convection doesn't heat the surface: it cools it, carrying heat up to where the air can radiate it away. The lapse rate is a cap, not a stove. And without infrared absorbers there is nothing to cap: 255 K. Venus shows the same at scale: 1603 K with radiation alone, 735 K with convection, in The Venus Test.

Dividing by 4 is not a flat Earth

The Sun lights Earth's disc, πR², and Earth radiates from its whole sphere, 4πR²: the ratio is 4. The 4 is the sphere.

Build the sphere from rings

Cut the globe into rings around the Sun–Earth line, each one a thin slice of a cone, and add them up. The light they catch together is always the light on their shadow. Their surface grows as the rings get thinner. Watch the ratio.

Surface of all the rings
Surface ÷ shadow
Sunlight per m² of surface, on average

Ring k runs between the points of the circle at θk = kπ/N and θk+1 from the point under the Sun; its surface is π(rk + rk+1) × s, with r = R sin θ and s = 2R sin(π/2N). The light it catches is the light on its shadow, π(rk+1² − rk²) × 960.9 W/m² (for a ring across the day–night line, only its lit part), so the lit rings together always catch 960.9 W/m² × πR². All the rings together: 4πR² × cos(π/2N), which goes to 4πR². In the unrolled strip each ring's width is its surface and its height the sunlight per m² on it: the shaded area is the light caught, the total length is the surface. Archimedes, On the Sphere and Cylinder, third century BC: the surface of a sphere equals that of the cylinder wrapped around it, 2πR × 2R = 4πR².

So 1361 × (1 − 0.294) / 4 = 240 W/m² is the sunlight absorbed, averaged over the sphere. It is bookkeeping on a round Earth, not a claim that Earth is flat or evenly lit. No heat stored: each spot radiates what it receives, (1 − s) × 960.9 × cos θ on the day side, θ being the Sun's angle from overhead, plus s × 240.2 W/m² everywhere, s being the share spread evenly; 960.9 W/m² is 1361 × (1 − 0.294). The horizontal axis is area, so the average temperature is the average height of the curve, and the average of σT⁴ is always 240.2 W/m². On an uneven planet the average of T is below the fourth root of the average of T⁴, so below 255 K. With nothing spread, the night side is at 0 K: a body that stores heat, like the Moon, is less extreme, and still averages below its black-body temperature. Even reflecting nothing, the even planet would be at 278 K.

A planet with no infrared absorbers, cut into six zones of equal area. The ceiling

Each zone sends ε σT⁴ to space; the six together must average 240.2 W/m², what the planet absorbs. The ceiling is the temperature of the even planet, (240.2 / εσ)1/4: 255.1 K, or 258.4 K with emissivity 0.95. The average of T can never exceed the fourth root of the average of T⁴ (the power-mean inequality), and equals it only when every zone has the same temperature. So no pattern of temperatures, however it comes about, lifts a planet with no infrared absorbers above the ceiling. The real Earth averages 288 K.

Mars has 25 times more CO₂ above each square metre than Earth, and a greenhouse effect about a sixth of Earth's. Three reasons:

  • Pressure. Each absorption line widens with pressure. At 6 millibar the lines are narrow and the surface's infrared slips out between them; at 1 bar they are broader; at 92 bar the band is closed.
  • Water. Mars has almost no water vapour, which on Earth does about half of the greenhouse effect and amplifies whatever CO₂ does.
  • Distance. Mars is cold first of all because it is far from the Sun: it absorbs 110 W/m², less than half of Earth's 240. Its black-body temperature is 210 K before any atmosphere.

The share of CO₂ in the air says little. The amount, the pressure it sits at and the water around it decide. Same CO₂ physics on three planets: about 5 K, 33 K, about 510 K.

Two centuries of the same physics

  1. 1824Joseph Fourier: sunlight gets through the air more easily than the ground's "dark heat" gets back out, so the air keeps Earth warmer.
  2. 1856Eunice Foote: in sunlight, a glass cylinder of CO₂ heats more than one of ordinary air, and stays hot longer. An atmosphere of that gas would give to our earth a high temperature.
  3. 1859John Tyndall measures in the laboratory that water vapour and CO₂ absorb and emit infrared, while oxygen and nitrogen hardly do.
  4. 1879–1884Josef Stefan and Ludwig Boltzmann: a body radiates σT⁴.
  5. 1896Svante Arrhenius: the first calculation of how much warmer Earth gets with more CO₂.
  6. 1900Max Planck: the spectrum of thermal radiation, the curves on this page.
  7. 1964Syukuro Manabe and Robert Strickler: radiation and convection together. Convection cools the surface; see "Radiation alone would be hotter".
  8. 1967Manabe and Richard Wetherald: doubling CO₂ warms the surface by about 2.4 K. Manabe shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physics for modelling Earth's climate.
  9. 1989Raval and Ramanathan: the greenhouse effect measured from satellites, as the infrared trapped by the air.
  10. 2001Harries et al.: Earth's infrared spectrum seen from space in 1970 and in 1997. The difference shows the greenhouse effect growing, gas by gas.
  11. 2015Feldman et al.: CO₂'s infrared reaching the ground, measured growing by 0.2 W/m² per decade.
  12. 2021Loeb et al.: Earth's energy imbalance roughly doubled from 2005 to 2019.

Objections

"It's the pressure: 1 bar of air keeps the surface warm."

Same 1 bar, same lapse rate, air that doesn't absorb infrared: 255 K. Press "Pressure only". Pressure decides how the air is arranged above the surface; it can't make the surface send more to space than Earth absorbs. A pressure that doesn't change does no work: see "If compression paid the bill".

"PV = nRT: the pressure sets the temperature."

The ideal gas law links pressure, density and temperature at each point; it doesn't say which one causes the others. At sea-level pressure, 1013 hPa, air at 288 K has a density of 1.225 kg/m³; at 255 K, 1.384 kg/m³. Both obey the law. Which temperature the surface gets is set by the energy ledger: what it absorbs and what it can send to space. The law then gives the density.

"−18 °C sits at the average height of the air, about 5 km: the lapse rate does the rest."

On Earth the middle of the mass (5.5 km) and the emission level (5.1 km) happen to be close, so the rule almost works. On Venus they are 10.4 and 71 km, and the same rule misses by 434 K. See "The 5 km coincidence".

"CO₂ is only 0.04% of the air."

0.04% of a lot of air. Above every square metre of Earth there are about 10 tonnes of air and 6.6 kg of CO₂. What matters for absorbing infrared is how much absorber there is, and at what pressure, not its share of the air: see "The Mars test". In the published attribution CO₂ is about 20% of the greenhouse effect.

"Water vapour does it all, not CO₂."

Water vapour and clouds do most of the absorbing, about 75%. But water condenses: its amount follows the temperature. In the GISS model, removing CO₂ and the other non-condensing gases makes the water rain out too, and the global mean falls to −21 °C in 50 years. See "Who does what".

"Cold air can't warm a warmer surface."

It doesn't heat it: heat, the net flow, always goes from the warm surface up. What the air does is send back 333 W/m² of infrared, so the surface loses less. The surface balances what it emits with what it absorbs, from the Sun and from the air. Take the air's share away and the net loss jumps; the surface cools to 255 K.

"Back radiation doesn't exist: it has never been measured."

It is measured every minute. Pyrgeometers at surface stations around the world record it day and night, and the global average is estimated at 333 to 342 W/m². Spectrometers looking up see it with the shape of its sources, the CO₂ band at 15 µm and the water vapour bands. From 2000 to 2010, two of them, in Oklahoma and in Alaska, measured the CO₂ part of it growing by 0.2 W/m² per decade, while CO₂ rose by 22 ppm (Feldman et al. 2015).

"The greenhouse effect is just a theory: nobody has ever seen it at work."

Everyone has. A cloudy night stays warmer than a clear one: the clouds absorb the ground's infrared and send part of it back. Measured, they add on average 28 W/m² to the infrared coming down, from 314 W/m² under clear skies to 342 (Wild et al. 2019). Dry deserts show the other side: little water vapour overhead, little infrared back, and the ground cools fast after sunset. Same Sun by day, different nights: what changes is the absorber overhead.

"Wood's 1909 experiment proved there is no greenhouse effect."

Wood warmed two boxes in the Sun, one under glass and one under rock salt, which lets infrared through, and found them almost equally warm: a greenhouse stays warm mainly because its lid stops the warm air from rising away. That is right, and it's why "greenhouse effect" is a poor name. But Earth has no lid. As a whole it can lose heat only by radiating to space, and it does so from about 5 km up, where the air is colder: that is the effect measured at the top, the 156 W/m² that never reach space. Wood himself wrote that he did "not pretend to have gone very deeply into the matter".

"It's the Sun."

The Sun supplies the 240 W/m² that Earth absorbs, and Earth sends the same back. The Moon, at the same distance, absorbs 26% more sunlight and is colder. See "The Moon test". And the stratosphere tells the two apart: a brighter Sun would warm it, CO₂ cools it, and it has cooled. See "CO₂ or the Sun?".

"You tuned the second run to give 288 K."

Yes: its height and slope are read from the measured profile, so it matches Earth by construction. It is there to show the shape. The proof doesn't use it: it uses the surface emission, the energy that leaves Earth and the sunlight the surface absorbs, all measured.

"The CO₂ band is saturated: adding more changes nothing."

The centre of the band is: there, more CO₂ changes little near the ground. But Earth radiates the band to space from aloft, and more CO₂ lifts that level to colder air: less leaves, the column warms. The forcing grows with the logarithm of the concentration and doesn't stop: about 3.7 W/m² for every doubling. See "Isn't CO₂ saturated?".

"CO₂ cools the planet: it radiates heat to space."

It does radiate to space, and that is where the warming comes from. Earth's CO₂ band leaves from high, cold air, so it sends out less than the warm surface would. Add CO₂ and the band leaves from higher still, colder: less goes out, and the air below warms (see "Isn't CO₂ saturated?"). In the middle and upper stratosphere, about 25 to 50 km, more CO₂ does cool: there it radiates more than it absorbs. Satellites measure that cooling, a human fingerprint that natural causes can't explain (Santer et al. 2023). A brighter Sun would warm the stratosphere, not cool it. See "CO₂ or the Sun?".

"Dividing the sunlight by 4 means a flat Earth with no night."

4 is the ratio between the disc that catches the sunlight, πR², and the sphere that radiates, 4πR². It averages energy; it doesn't say the temperature is even. And the even planet is the warmest case: day and night make the average colder, never warmer. Move the slider in "Dividing by 4".

"Mars is 95% CO₂ and freezing."

Mars has about 25 times more CO₂ per square metre than Earth, at 160 times lower pressure, with almost no water vapour, and it absorbs less than half the sunlight. Its greenhouse effect is about 5 K; Earth's is 33 K, Venus's about 510 K. The share of CO₂ in the air says little. See "The Mars test".

Method. Sunlight absorbed: 1361 × (1 − albedo) / 4. The emission level is a sphere of radius R + h radiating the same power, at Tem = Te (R / (R + h))½, R = 6371 km. Lapse rate 6.5 K/km, the U.S. Standard Atmosphere's troposphere. The surface is treated as black in the infrared, so σT⁴ at 288.15 K gives 391 W/m²; the measured 396 is higher because the surface is not uniform. This is a test of energy bookkeeping, not a climate model.
